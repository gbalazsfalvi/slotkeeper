# Slotkeeper v0.2 signed hold admission

**Status** · The owner selected issuer-backed permits. This detailed contract and its defaults are proposed, pending owner approval, and unimplemented.

## Scope and selected approach

Gate fresh `POST /api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/holds` writes with one short-lived ML-DSA-65 permit. The browser uses the existing same-origin route, scoped session, CSRF header, JSON body, and `Idempotency-Key`. The permit travels only between servers. The booking transaction enforces current membership, room and slot rules, the two-live-hold allowance, and existing SQL constraints. Confirmation, cancellation, and booking queries keep their v1 contracts. The local reference uses owned fixtures, two workspaces, two booking replicas, and one separate issuer. [Slotkeeper HTTP contract](../../API.md), [Slotkeeper threat model](../../THREAT_MODEL.md)

The selected path adds one dependency for new holds and lets us measure admission and concurrency on an existing business action. The alternative, signed waitlist offers, would also govern allocation order under a surge. It needs bounded enrollment, ordering, expired offer reassignment, and a waiting flow. The options note records those trade-offs, primary sources, and the observed Java 21 provider interoperability check. Production deployment, online key rotation, proof of work, and AI-based admission are outside this slice. [Research options and feasibility](../../research/2026-11-18-pqc-admission-options.md)

## Authority and permit

Each booking replica authenticates the browser session and CSRF token, resolves the path workspace through current membership, and derives an immutable actor and workspace principal. It validates the hold and idempotency key using the v1 rules. Both replicas call the issuer over a private channel using explicitly pinned, mutually authenticated service identities. The issuer accepts only these callers and the fixed hold action and audience. Browser fields and headers cannot set the service identity, actor, or permitted scope.

The ingress establishes identity. The issuer independently decides whether its durable budget for that actor and workspace allows a grant. The issuer holds the only private signing key and can write its own ledger. Its database identity can invoke a bounded, argument-free capability to obtain the shared booking decision time. It has no direct clock update or reservation write privilege. Booking replicas have pinned issuer public keys and booking privileges. An abusive session holder can request approvals only under their current scope and the issuer's limits. A compromised ingress can assert identities it is trusted to derive, a stolen issuer key can forge approvals, and a compromised booking writer can bypass application policy within its database privileges.

Use compact JWS with a protected `typ` of `slotkeeper-hold-permit+jwt`, `alg` of `ML-DSA-65`, and configured `kid`. Sign fixed `iss` and `aud`, the permit profile `slotkeeper.hold-admission.v1`, actor UUID, workspace UUID, `CREATE_HOLD`, normalized typed `roomId` and `startAt`, SHA-384 of the exact validated idempotency key, and `iat`, `exp`, and a random 128-bit `jti`. Use integer NumericDates. Round the shared decision timestamp down to epoch seconds for `iat` and set `exp` to at most 30 seconds later, giving at most 30 decision seconds of validity. Verify the typed intent against the authorized request. Require the fixed type, profile, local key configuration, and audience. Reject `alg: none`, duplicate fields, and unexpected headers, including `jku` and embedded `jwk`. Use RFC 9964's empty ML-DSA context. No request can choose trust material. [JWS RFC 7515](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7515.html), [ML-DSA RFC 9964 §5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9964.html#section-5), [JWT guidance RFC 8725](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8725.html)

## Admission and writes

- Start with three distinct approved intents per actor within each workspace in any ten minutes. Atomically check and debit the budget using durable decision time. Key grants by actor, workspace, operation, and idempotency digest. A changed body under a retained key conflicts. Reserve the claims and nonce before signing. An ambiguous issuer response or signing failure can recover the same live grant without another charge. Allow one renewal within the original ten-minute window if the receiver has no committed receipt and the original envelope expired. Reuse the same `jti` and immutable intent and key binding, with a fresh `iat` and `exp` bounded by the original window. Renewal consumes no additional approval and cannot enable another effect because both envelopes use the same redemption key. Deciding to use a new idempotency key creates a new intent and charge. Charges survive cancellation, abandonment, signing failure, and later capacity conflicts. Measure how often legitimate users exhaust their budget while trying competing slots. [RFC 6585 §4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6585.html#section-4)
- Keep v1 request and rate limits. Cap the internal encoded permit at 8 KiB before decoding. Reject malformed, oversized, or wrong-policy requests before expensive verification. Start with at most two crypto jobs per booking replica and two at the issuer, one reserved position per fixture workspace in each process. Use a zero-length wait queue and a two-second total issuer call deadline. Return `429` with `Retry-After` for an exhausted actor budget and a bounded `503` for unavailable issuer, ledger, clock, or crypto capacity. Reserved positions and short request limits bound workspace compute pressure without a shared long-window approval quota. There is no distributed rollback of a committed issuance charge after a booking failure. Bound ledger retention and preserve decisions needed for live grants, renewal, and rolling accounting. [OWASP API4](https://api-security.owasp.org/editions/2023/en/0xa4-unrestricted-resource-consumption/)
- Verify the signature and request binding outside booking locks. After the actor, room and slot locks and any applicable reservation lock, use the existing nondecreasing PostgreSQL booking clock to require `decisionTime < exp`. Recheck current authority and booking eligibility at the decision. Atomically commit unique `(issuer, jti)` redemption, hold, receipt, and event. Retain redemptions for at least ten decision minutes after redemption, covering the latest possible renewal. The existing clock can pause during a backward wall-clock adjustment, so grant lifetime is measured in decision time. [Durable booking time](../../decisions/0005-durable-booking-time.md), [PostgreSQL conflict handling](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-insert.html)
- An exact retry with a committed receipt returns the current authorized reservation even after permit expiry or issuer outage. It applies no second booking effect. Current authentication, CSRF, scope, raw attempt admission, and authorization to read the reservation still apply. The clock and counters may advance. Recheck for a matching committed receipt when resolving a write race. Keep v1 `409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT` for a different fingerprint. If a receipt is absent, any write requires a valid unspent grant. This differs deliberately from the PQC Migration Lab's verify-every-retry contract.

Use a static trust set during operation. For key replacement or revocation, stop accepting new hold writes, stop issuance, drain or abort old writes across **all** receivers, and restart with the new trust set and corresponding signing key. Fresh holds are unavailable during cutover. The effective cutoff is established by completing that sequence. Immediate revocation during live writes needs another design.

## Evidence and approval

Use owned PostgreSQL fixtures and independent final-state checks. Cover altered actor, workspace, action, slot, audience, idempotency binding, and signature. Reject wrong-profile, classical-only, unknown-key, and removed-key grants. Check strict post-lock expiry, concurrent redemption across replicas, lost response after commit, expired retry with a receipt, a revoked member retry, and issuer retry and renewal after a committed charge. Include renewal racing an already committed booking and prove zero extra charge or booking effect. For a 100-actor same-slot check, require evidence that all counted attempts passed permit and admission checks and reached the booking decision. Report actual lock overlap and early rejections. Confirm that new writes fail closed and that authorized exact receipt replays still work during issuer failure.

Compare v1 with a local 2×2 experiment varying issuance budget on/off and Ed25519/ML-DSA-65 on the same topology. Keep all v1 controls enabled in every arm and keep comparison modes confined to the harness. Include benign bursts, cancellation churn, sustained hold expiry and reacquisition, and many valid actors with separate fixtures. Stress one workspace while legitimate users book uncontended slots in the other. Freeze offered load, seeds, repetitions, and gates before evaluation. Report eligible legitimate success by deadline and false positives, excluding genuine slot conflicts from that denominator. Record unconfirmed slot-minutes, p95 latency, CPU and allocation per stage, and wire bytes. Proposed gates include under 1% benign false positives, at least 95% of quiet-workspace baseline success, and a predeclared occupancy reduction and latency budget. V0.1's two-live-hold cap already limits each actor, so incremental abuse reduction is an empirical question. Many valid actors can still accumulate holds within their respective limits and consume workspace inventory.

First review the trust and retry contracts. Then agree on the proposed limits and workload used to set acceptance thresholds. After detailed approval, prepare the implementation plan, establish a provider conformance fixture, and build only the gated hold path. The release evidence must show transaction correctness and measured abuse and performance effects for the recorded workload.
